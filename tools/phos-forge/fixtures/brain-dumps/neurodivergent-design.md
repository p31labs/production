the thing that makes P31 different and I keep forgetting to say it — it's not an AI product with accessibility bolted on. it's an assistive technology product that happens to use AI

spoon theory isn't a metaphor here, it's a variable. 0-5. at 5 you get the full interface. at 3, motion-scale drops to 0.6. at 1-2, density collapses, disclosure shrinks, the interface gets QUIETER. at 0, crisis mode, the live dashboard replaces itself with a rest stop. the interface RESPONDS to capacity

nobody does this. every product assumes you're at 100% every day. that assumption is the disability. the tool-task mismatch — using a micrometer as a hammer. the SOULSAFE doc says it: "the insight becomes the action, rather than the insight informing the action"

the design system constraints — 48px touch floor, 56px recommended. motion-scale multiplied, never disabled (collapsing to 0.01 is a calm pause, not a hard stop). OKLCH tokens, contrast audit. data-spoons on the html element so every component can adapt without JS branching

contradiction: is this a niche or is this the future? neurodivergent-first design is assistive tech. but the principles — respect capacity, reduce load, respond to state — are what EVERYONE needs. the ADHD brain and the exhausted neurotypical brain want the same interface. I think it's the future wearing a niche's clothes

gap: no data on retention. does a spoon-aware interface actually keep people using it on their low days, or does it just make the low days less bad? unmeasured

the family thing — this isn't a solo tool. it's children + parent + teen portals, shared mesh, the "lights on the street" presence. a caregiver can see the kid's state without surveilling. consent-based, DID-bound, no telemetry. the sovereign stack paper calls it "cognitive insulation"

the quiet interface idea — while the agents work, the trace is calm. when results arrive, the trace collapses to a summary. you see the work if you want it, you don't if you don't. progressive disclosure as an accessibility feature, not a UX nicety

what's the ONE thing? the interface meets you where you are. not where the design team assumes you are. spoon-aware, calm-mode, family-first. it's not a feature, it's the premise
