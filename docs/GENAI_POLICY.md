# P31 Labs — GenAI Use Policy

**Status:** adopted · **Effective:** 2026-09-24 · **Corrected:** 2026-09-24 · **Applies to:** all P31 Labs projects and public surfaces, whether or not NLnet-funded.

---

## The position

P31 Labs uses generative AI as a **tool**, not a collaborator. The human maintainer directs the architecture, makes the design decisions, discovers the bug findings, and reviews every output before it enters the repository. AI generates substantial code and prose under that human direction; the human owns and is accountable for the result.

This is the "AI-assisted" tier of the FAIA attribution standard (human-created / AI-assisted / AI-generated). It is consistent with NLnet Foundation's policy: AI-assisted work with honest disclosure is eligible; work *mostly generated* by LLMs (vibecoding) — accepted by a human without substantive review or creative direction — is not.

**The honest scope of this claim is per-artifact, not project-level.** Design is human-directed. Implementation is AI-assisted. Both statements are true, and the policy says both.

---

## Where the human directed (creative control)

- **Architecture.** The K4 topology, the pre-execution gate (`guardAgentEvent`), the refusal sidecar, the Merkle batch anchoring. These are human design decisions.
- **Bug discoveries.** The three MCP ecosystem findings (stdio-only registry, JSON-RPC envelope requirement, Durable Object forked chains) were discovered by a human running real clients against real servers and interpreting failures.
- **Verification strategy.** The *what* to verify — the chain proof, the canon-language guard, the dead-email guard, the drift check — is a human decision.
- **Standards alignment scope.** The judgment calls (Art. 12 applies to high-risk systems, not P31; SCITT is Designed, not certified) are human.

## Where AI assisted (generated, then human-reviewed and owned)

- Implementation code for the above was substantially AI-generated, then reviewed, understood, tested, and owned by the human.
- CSS, boilerplate, documentation drafting, test-case suggestions, consistent-formatting passes.

AI did **not** direct the architecture, decide scope, or discover bugs. It generated implementations and drafts that the human reviewed and owns.

## The two corpora (research vs. code)

P31 produces two distinct bodies of work, and the authorship claim is different for each.

**The research corpus** (the P31 Research Series, published on Zenodo with DOIs under ORCID 0009-0002-2492-9079) is **human-authored in the strong sense.** The ideas, the synthesis, the corrections, and the lived substrate are the operator's. The signature of human authorship is in the papers themselves:

- **Paper IV corrects Paper I in print.** The non-planarity claim about K₄ is withdrawn and the argument rebuilt on simplicial volumetric enclosure (β₂ = 1). A model does not do this.
- **Paper III maintains the science/metaphor boundary explicitly** ("'mental wormholes' are evocative but imprecise"; "'quantum brain' is rejected by mainstream science").
- **Paper XII records specific hardware constraints** — the PSRAM firewall on GPIO 33–37, the AXS15231B display reset sequence — that only a person who wrote firmware against the actual silicon could document.
- **Paper XIV is genealogical and specific** — the operator's 1925–2025 generational cycle is not background, it is the substrate.
- **Paper III's lived clinical history** ("22 years of 'anxiety' was a voltage problem") is not generated prose.

AI assisted the research corpus with drafting, structure, formatting, and prose passes. The author of record is the human.

**The code corpus** (the infrastructure, the marketplace, the site) is **human-directed, AI-assisted implementation.** The design decisions are human; the implementation was substantially AI-generated, then reviewed, understood, tested, and owned by the human.

Both statements are true. The research corpus is human-authored; the code corpus is human-directed with AI-assisted implementation. Neither is "mostly generated by an LLM without human creative control."

## Honest disclosure (this is what NLnet reviewers should rely on)

P31 did **not** keep contemporaneous prompt-by-prompt logs. The honest record is:

1. The public git history and session transcripts in the repository.
2. The per-artifact statement in `docs/LOOM_PROMPT_PROVENANCE_LOG.md` (which is a disclosure, not a constructed prompt log).
3. The human maintainer's ability to explain any design decision, any bug fix, and any line of code.

We do not attribute this work to a single model in policy documents, because the work spanned the canonical agent-role model (Opus/Sonnet/Gemini/DeepSeek per the Triad of Cognition) and session orchestration. Where a transcript or commit records a specific model, that record is the source; policy files name the roles and defer per-session attribution to the transcripts rather than inventing a relationship.

If a funder requires a prompt-provenance log, we will discuss what is actually available — the honest answer is that contemporaneous prompt logs were not kept. We state that rather than present a fabricated log.

---

## Disclosure requirements

1. **Grant applications.** Any NLnet (or other funder) application discloses AI assistance explicitly, names the tools used where known, and points to this policy + the disclosure statement.
2. **Public narrative.** The human is always the author. AI is never listed as a co-author, co-signer, or "assisted-by" trailer on commits.
3. **License compliance.** All AI-assisted output is reviewed to ensure it does not reproduce copyrighted or license-incompatible material.
4. **Model naming.** Model names are only stated where a transcript or commit actually records them — never invented.

---

## The honest line in public narrative

Public statements describe the relationship as *"an AI as my only tool"* — never *"an AI as my only collaborator."* The distinction is intentional: "collaborator" implies shared creative control; "tool" preserves human authorship.

---

## Human accountability

Every line of code in P31 repositories has been read, understood, tested, and is explainable by the human maintainer. This is the standard the Linux kernel and Kubernetes require: the human holds the DCO liability; the AI cannot.

*This policy is published per NLnet Foundation's GenAI policy (v1.1 of 26 January 2026), which recommends projects publish their own policy.*
