**Controlling facts**: hearing **October 15, 2026**, Superior Court of Camden County (Woodbine); case **Johnson v. Johnson, 2025CV936**; pro se; ~70+ days separated from your children.

### Week 1 (Sep 22–28): the immediate motions

**Day 1–2 — Habeas corpus petition.** File a Petition for Writ of Habeas Corpus and Emergency Return of Child (O.C.G.A. § 9-14-2) in Camden County Superior Court. It enforces the last valid written custody/visitation order — it does not modify custody. E-file via **PeachCourt** (`peachcourt.com`); filing fee **$206**, request **in forma pauperis** (qualifies at $5 assets / zero income).

**Day 3–4 — Motion to set aside the void consent order** (O.C.G.A. § 9-11-60(d) fraud/accident/mistake; § 9-11-60(f) void attacks any time). Basis: the Oct 23, 2025 order bears terminated-counsel East's signature. The statutory anchor is **O.C.G.A. § 15-19-5/§ 15-19-6** (an attorney needs special authority to bind a client); *Lewis v. Uselton* (202 Ga. App. 875 (1992)) supports "special authority required," not "discharge voids an order" — the void-ab-initio application must be argued separately and confirmed with GLSP. Nonamendable defect.

**Day 5–7 — Motion to dismiss the Third Contempt** (Entry 92, filed Apr 4). The order it prosecutes wasn't signed/filed until Apr 14 (Entry 104, nunc pro tunc). O.C.G.A. § 9-11-58(b): no judgment is effective for any purpose until signed + filed. *Beard v. Beard*; *Titelman v. Stedman* (O.C.G.A. § 9-11-58(b)): a nunc pro tunc order corrects the record; it does not create retroactive liability. You cannot be in contempt of an order that didn't exist on the alleged violation date.
