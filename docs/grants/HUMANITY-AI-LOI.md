## 1. Who we are

P31 Labs is a US 501(c)(3) public-interest technology organization. We build
**sovereign, privacy-preserving software for neurodivergent people and the
families who care for them**, and we give the design and source away. We do not
sell the software; grants fund the work so it stays free. Everything we ship is
released **open-source** (Apache-2.0/MIT) so the community owns it outright —
never a paywall, never a vendor lock-in. Our model follows public-good
precedents — Freenet, Benetech, Civitas ID — not software vendors.

The organization is led and operated by a neurodivergent founder and a small
family crew. The community we serve is not a market segment to us; it is us.

## 2. The community we center

**Neurodivergent people and family caregivers — a household, not an app.**

Most "care AI" is built *for* disabled people and *sold to* institutions. It
optimizes for the provider's billing, not the person's dignity. We build the
inverse: tools the household governs, where the AI is accountable to the person
it mediates care for.

This is the spine. Education, the labor economy, and arts and culture are the
breadth around it — because a caregiver's day *is* a curriculum, a job, and a
creative practice at once.

## 3. What the community would build (and lead)

**A household-governed care companion, with the community as the governing body.**

- **Care economy, human-scaled.** A spoon-capacity model treats energy as a
  scarce resource: on low-capacity days the software does less, not more, and a
  crisis mode stops motion entirely. A care ledger records the small, real work
  of a household — medication, appointments, sensory breaks — as visible,
  dignified units rather than surveillance.
- **Privacy by construction, not policy.** Every person in a household is
  represented by a **deterministic "pickle name"** — a privacy-preserving label
  derived from a seed, so *the street never shows a human name, pickle labels
  only*. Identities are self-sovereign (W3C DIDs, selective-disclosure
  credentials), and the cryptographic core is post-quantum-ready, so a family's
  data is not exposed when today's crypto is broken.
- **Agent governance.** The AI that proposes a care action must *sign* for it
  with a verifiable, hash-chained approval — there is a ledger of what the agent
  did and why, legible to a non-technical caregiver. Eleven governance gates,
  each proven able to fail (a planted violation is caught), are the **"audit
  approaches that ensure AI systems work as intended"** the call names — run
  continuously, not written as an aspiration.

**Who leads:** a **Community Advisory Council** of neurodivergent adults and
family caregivers holds decision rights over what gets built, what data is
touched, and what the AI may never do. The grant pays for community
participation, not just engineering.

## 5. What the call excludes, and how we comply

Humanity AI supports **community-led** work and **excludes** (a) adoption of
specific AI products and (b) research that does not advance community
leadership. This LOI therefore does **not** request funds to buy or deploy a
vendor AI product, and does not propose research for its own sake. Every
deliverable is (1) built by the community and (2) owned by the community, with
the grant funding the community's **capacity to govern** the AI in its life.

We hold ourselves to the standard we ask of the AI. Our own gates recently
caught — and we fixed — three defects in our post-quantum KEM: a bespoke combiner
that was not spec-compliant X-Wing, a public endpoint that leaked a private key,
and a signature verifier that failed open. That is evidence of self-audit, not a
claim of perfection (see `docs/grants/CASE-STUDY.md` §5).
