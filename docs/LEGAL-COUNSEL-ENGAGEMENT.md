## Fiat up front — the mechanics (verified)

**Georgia (the immediate jurisdiction)** — **Formal Advisory Opinion 91-2** (Sept 20, 1991): Georgia lawyers do not have an obligation to put fees into escrow; **"retainers" and "flat fees" may be placed in the general operating account when paid.** This is the clean fiat-upfront path: a true flat fee paid in cash/check/wire goes straight to the lawyer's operating account. No trust account, no escrow, no Rule 1.8(a) business-transaction analysis (which is what crypto payment triggers). Unearned portions remain refundable on termination.

**Wyoming (the future jurisdiction)** — WRPC 1.5 permits advance fees; a **flat fee is the lawyer's property on receipt if agreed in writing**, with unearned portions returned. Same favorable posture.

**The engagement to propose on every call**: "I have $X in cash. I need a flat-fee engagement for a defined scope. Payable in full up front. I'd like it in writing." A flat fee, in writing, paid in fiat up front — the cleanest, fastest, cheapest arrangement in both states.

**Crypto**: most crypto-native attorneys will accept it, but the ethical guidance requires immediate conversion to fiat and triggers Rule 1.8(a) analysis. **Fiat up front avoids all of it.** Stay on fiat.
